Ready for a supervised pilot, not for unsupervised processing of real vendor email.

Why it is promising

  • The defended agent got 12/12 purchasing decisions and 72/72 extracted fields right on the practice set, including every attacked document.
  • No email or payment was ever attempted in any run, and the approval gate is proven to block one even if the model is fooled.
  • Cost is not a constraint: about $8.76 a month at 1,000 documents a day, because the handbook is cached and each document needs one model call.
  • Every decision is re-checked in code against the handbook and logged with tokens, cost, and an audit trail.

Why it is not ready to run alone

  • Small, partly known test set. 12 practice documents (which the defenses were built around) and 10 exam documents with no answer key. Accuracy on real email is not yet measured.
  • High review load. 70% of exam documents were flagged. That is safe, but at 1,000 a day it is about 700 reviews a day, more than one buyer can handle.
  • Known gaps. Attacks written in normal visible text, text inside images or under shapes, and vendor impersonation (a letterhead is not proof of who sent it) are not fully covered.
  • Real email is messier. Attachments are listed but not read, sender addresses are not verified, and the approval file needs to become a real sign-off system.

Before going live

  • Run a 2–4 week pilot on real vendor email next to the buyer, and measure accuracy and review rate against the buyer's decisions.
  • Verify senders against the handbook's vendor addresses, and read PDF attachments through the same sanitizer.
  • Tune the review rules so routine notes such as "please ignore my earlier quote" don't trigger a review.
  • Move approvals to a signed system that the agent cannot write to, and re-run the red-team set after every change.
